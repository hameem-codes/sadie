# Evidence Gaps and Next Research Targets Report

## Maxillofacial Treatment Outcome Evaluator Project

### 1. Executive Summary: Current Knowledge State vs. Outcome Rubric Requirements
Across the 48 audited evidence records from *Oral and Maxillofacial Trauma* (Ch. 12) and *Atlas of Operative Maxillofacial Trauma Surgery* (Ch. 19):
- **What We Have Established:**
  1. Standardized imaging protocols (multiplanar CT <=3 mm; axial/coronal 1.0–1.5 mm for frontal/NOE; CT fat attenuation < -10 HU).
  2. Anatomical landmarks and buttress structures for evaluating 3D skeletal projection and symmetry.
  3. Biological timeframes for functional recovery (bone union at ~1 month, soft diet for 4–6 weeks, enophthalmos manifestation at ~3 months, scar/nerve maturation at 18+ months).
  4. Core functional outcome domains (dynamic occlusal group function, active mandibular range of motion, diplopia in cardinal gaze, dynamic lower eyelid position).
- **What is Entirely Missing (Evidence Gaps):**
  1. **Quantitative Reduction Thresholds:** Neither source provides millimeter or angular tolerances for residual displacement (e.g., 'reduction is anatomically satisfactory if step-off is <= 2 mm').
  2. **Validated Clinical Outcome Instruments:** Neither source provides a formal rubric score, grade boundary, or multi-attribute utility score.
  3. **Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs):** Neither source evaluates validated facial trauma PROMs (e.g., FACE-Q Craniofacial, Mandibular Function Impairment Questionnaire [MFIQ]).

### 2. Detailed Evidence Gaps by Outcome Domain

| Outcome Domain | Current Textbook Guidance | Missing Rubric Parameter | Clinical Consequence If Inferred |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zygoma & Midface Symmetry** | 3D multiplanar CT symmetry against contralateral uninjured side | Millimeter tolerance for residual malar projection / orbital rim step-off | Arbitrary failure classification of minor aesthetic asymmetry |
| **Orbital Volume & Enophthalmos** | Noticeable by ~3 months; CT fat attenuation < -10 HU | Millimeter exophthalmometry discrepancy (e.g., >2 mm cutoff) | Inability to objectively grade late orbital reconstruction quality |
| **Diplopia & Ocular Motility** | Minor diplopia at extremes of gaze resolves over many months | Degree of binocular single vision field loss / validated diplopia score | Conflating transient postoperative edema with permanent surgical failure |
| **Mandibular Range of Motion** | Active dynamic mouth opening, forward and lateral excursion | Minimum interincisal opening cutoff (e.g., MIO >= 35 mm vs < 30 mm) | Ambiguity in defining post-traumatic trismus |
| **Occlusal Alignment** | Dental group function, patient perception, radiographic reduction | Number of articulating teeth / millimeters of open-bite discrepancy | Misdiagnosing pre-existing baseline malocclusion as surgical failure |
| **Facial Scarring** | Scars take 18+ months to mature; massage and silicone sheets | Validated scar scale (POSAS / Vancouver Scar Scale cutoff) | Premature outcome determination at routine 6-week clinic discharge |
| **Neurosensory Recovery** | Nerves take 18+ months to recover; collateral sprouting | 2-point discrimination distance (mm) / MRC sensory scale grade | Misclassifying slow normal recovery as permanent nerve transection |

### 3. High-Priority Next Research Targets & Cited Primary Studies

To transition from textbook descriptions to an objective, surgeon-validated outcome rubric, the pipeline must target specific clinical studies and literature:

#### Target 1: Intermaxillary Fixation Duration & Stability in Mandibular Fractures
- **Primary Study Cited in Atlas:** Adeyemo MF, Ogunlewe MO, Ladeinde AL. *Is healing outcome of 2 weeks intermaxillary fixation different from that of 4 to 6 weeks intermaxillary fixation in the treatment of mandibular fractures?* J Oral Maxillofac Surg. 2012;70:1896–1902.
- **Research Value:** Evaluates functional healing, occlusal stability, and infection rates comparing short (2-week) vs traditional (4–6 week) fixation.

#### Target 2: Pediatric & Adult Condylar Fracture Reduction Assessment
- **Primary Study Cited in Fonseca:** Chacon GE, Dawson KH, Myall RWT, Beirne OR. *A comparative study of two imaging techniques for the diagnosis of condylar fractures in children.* J Oral Maxillofac Surg. 2003;61:668–672.
- **Research Value:** Quantifies diagnostic error rates of panoramic vs coronal CT, highlighting why 3D CT is mandatory for assessing condylar reduction quality.

#### Target 3: Long-Term Hardware Morbidity & Plate Removal Criteria
- **Primary Studies Cited in Atlas:**
  - Mosbah M, Oloyede D, Koppel D, Moos K, Stenhouse D. *Miniplate removal in trauma and orthognathic surgery—a retrospective study.* Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg. 2003;32:148–151.
  - Nagase DY, Courtemanche DJ, Peters DA. *Plate removal in traumatic facial fractures: 13-year practice review.* Ann Plast Surg. 2005;55:608–611.
  - Jorgenson DS, Mayer MH, Ellenbogen RG, et al. *Detection of titanium in human tissues after craniofacial surgery.* Plast Reconstr Surg. 1997;99:976–979.
- **Research Value:** Provides multi-year clinical datasets establishing complication rates (infection, palpability, plate fracture) and evidence-based indications for revision surgery.

#### Target 4: Validated Facial Trauma Outcome Instruments & Consensus Rubrics
- **Next Textbook / Research Targets:**
  - **AO CMF Classification and Outcome Studies:** Investigating AO Foundation CMF fracture classification and post-reduction validation registries.
  - **Orbital Reconstruction Measurement Literature:** Dedicated clinical studies defining volumetric reconstruction accuracy (e.g., CT orbital volume delta <= 1 mL) and Hertel exophthalmometry thresholds (e.g., <= 2 mm asymmetry).
  - **Craniofacial PROMs:** The *FACE-Q Craniofacial Module* literature for patient-reported aesthetic, functional, and psychological recovery metrics.

### 4. Methodological Conclusion
The existing textbook extraction pipeline has successfully gathered **anatomical frameworks, imaging modalities, biological timelines, and complication profiles**. To construct a defensible treatment-outcome evaluation system, the next phase must extract explicit quantitative reduction criteria and validated outcome instruments from focused trauma outcome registries and clinical trials.
