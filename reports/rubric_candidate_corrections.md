# Rubric Candidate Corrections & Scope Boundary Analysis

## Maxillofacial Fracture Treatment-Outcome Evaluator Project — Phase 3

### 1. Executive Summary: Evidence Grounding vs. Rubric Overinterpretation
A core objective of this project is to build a reliable, traceable, evidence-gathering foundation without prematurely inventing clinical scoring systems, arbitrary cutoffs, or unvalidated composite scores.

This report systematically reviews four proposed clinical implications that emerged during early literature synthesis. Each proposed rule is evaluated against the exact data reported in the primary studies to prevent rubric overinterpretation and unwarranted extrapolation.

### 3. Detailed Review of Proposed Implications

#### Implication 1: Residual ZMC Asymmetry of 1.5–2.0 mm as an Acceptance Boundary
- **Source Studies:**
  - Ho JPTF, Schreurs R, et al. (2016), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, 44(10):1706–1712 (`PAP-001` / `RES-EVD-001`).
  - Ho JPTF, Schreurs R, et al. (2017), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, 45(12):1927–1933 (`PAP-002` / `RES-EVD-002`).
- **What the Primary Literature Actually Reports:**
  - Ho et al. (2016) demonstrated the mathematical and clinical feasibility of using 3D CT mirroring and surface-based matching to calculate surface distances between facial halves.
  - Ho et al. (2017) examined **15 uninjured adult trauma controls** with normal facial skeletons and documented natural baseline asymmetry: **$\text{Mean Surface Distance (MSD)} = 0.14 \pm 0.12\text{ mm}$** (range $0.002\text{--}0.881\text{ mm}$) and **$\text{Root Mean Square Distance (RMSD)} = 0.87 \pm 0.21\text{ mm}$** (range $0.56\text{--}1.98\text{ mm}$).
- **Nature of the Overinterpretation:**
  - Proposing that any post-reduction ZMC with residual asymmetry $\le 1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ mm}$ must automatically be scored as "clinically acceptable" or "successful reduction" conflates an observational normal population range with a proven post-surgical outcome standard.
  - The $1.98\text{ mm}$ upper bound was the maximum physiological asymmetry observed in a small cohort ($n=15$) of uninjured Dutch patients. It was not derived from a clinical trial testing surgeon satisfaction or patient aesthetic perception.
- **Rubric Correction & Proper Scope:**
  - **Status:** `CANDIDATE COMPARATIVE BOUNDARY ONLY — NO EXPLICIT SUCCESS THRESHOLD REPORTED`.
  - **Rule Definition:** In an outcome evaluation system, a residual asymmetry $< 1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ mm}$ indicates that skeletal morphology falls within the range of natural human biological asymmetry. However, it cannot be decreed as a standalone binary passing score without clinical and patient validation.

#### Implication 2: Orbital Volume Difference of 1.0 mL as a Universal Success Threshold
- **Source Studies:**
  - Whitehouse RW, et al. (1994), *Br J Ophthalmol*, 78(8):618–620 (`PAP-003` / `RES-EVD-003`).
  - Zhang Z, et al. (2012), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, 70(1):68–73 (`PAP-004` / `RES-EVD-004`).
  - Bly RA, et al. (2013), *JAMA Facial Plast Surg*, 15(2):113–120 (`PAP-005` / `RES-EVD-005`).
- **What the Primary Literature Actually Reports:**
  - Whitehouse et al. (1994) established a **linear regression ratio**: each $1.0\text{ cm}^3$ ($1.0\text{ mL}$) increase in bony orbital volume corresponds to approximately **$0.8\text{ mm}$ of enophthalmos** ($r = 0.79, P < 0.001$).
  - Zhang et al. (2012) showed that volume alone is insufficient: only herniation **posterior to the vertical eyeball equator** significantly drives enophthalmos ($P < .05$).
  - Bly et al. (2013) demonstrated that computer-guided reconstruction achieved a mean postoperative volume difference of **$0.74\text{ mL}$** compared to $2.12\text{ mL}$ in conventional surgery ($P = .003$), lowering enophthalmos rates from $38\%$ to $9\%$.
- **Nature of the Overinterpretation:**
  - Treating $\le 1.0\text{ mL}$ as a "universal success threshold" misinterprets both biomechanics and clinical observation:
    1. A $1.0\text{ mL}$ expansion is mathematically predicted to yield $0.8\text{ mm}$ of enophthalmos, which is generally subclinical ($<2.0\text{ mm}$).
    2. However, if a $1.0\text{ mL}$ expansion is concentrated entirely posterior to the eyeball equator or accompanied by retrobulbar fat necrosis, clinically significant enophthalmos or diplopia may still occur.
    3. Conversely, a patient with a $1.2\text{ mL}$ volume difference anteriorly may have zero enophthalmos and perfect ocular motility.
- **Rubric Correction & Proper Scope:**
  - **Status:** `CONTINUOUS PREDICTIVE PARAMETER ONLY — NO UNIVERSAL THRESHOLD REPORTED`.
  - **Rule Definition:** CT orbital volume delta ($\Delta\text{mL}$) is a continuous predictor of globe position ($0.8\text{ mm}/\text{mL}$). A delta $\le 1.0\text{ mL}$ serves as an empirical target in computer-assisted surgery, but cannot serve as an isolated binary threshold of surgical success. Ocular motility and clinical globe projection must be independently assessed.

#### Implication 3: Maximum Interincisal Opening (MIO) > 40 mm as a Universal Success Threshold
- **Source Studies:**
  - Eckelt U, et al. (2006), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, 34(5):306–314 (`PAP-006` / `RES-EVD-006`).
  - Ellis E 3rd, et al. (2000), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, 58(3):260–268 (`PAP-007` / `RES-EVD-007`).
  - Adeyemi MF, et al. (2012), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, 70(8):1896–1902 (`PAP-008` / `RES-EVD-008`).
- **What the Primary Literature Actually Reports:**
  - Eckelt et al. (2006) reported that in displaced condylar process fractures, operative treatment achieved a **mean MIO of $46.5\text{ mm}$**, whereas closed treatment achieved a **mean MIO of $40.9\text{ mm}$** ($P = 0.001$).
  - Adeyemi et al. (2012) reported that patients managed with 2 weeks of IMF achieved earlier recovery of normal mouth opening compared to 4–6 weeks of IMF.
- **Nature of the Overinterpretation:**
  - Setting $\text{MIO} > 40\text{ mm}$ as a universal threshold for surgical success creates two clinical errors:
    1. In Eckelt's trial, **both treatment groups achieved mean openings above $40\text{ mm}$** ($46.5\text{ mm}$ vs $40.9\text{ mm}$). Declaring $>40\text{ mm}$ as the boundary would falsely classify closed treatment as an equal success, masking the significant differences in lateral excursion, protrusion ($7.3\text{ mm}$ vs $4.7\text{ mm}$), and terminal deviation ($19\%$ vs $66\%$).
    2. Baseline pre-morbid interincisal opening varies significantly by sex, age, height, and dentition (e.g., normal adult ranges from $35\text{ mm}$ to $55\text{ mm}$). A fixed cutoff of $40\text{ mm}$ would penalize patients whose uninjured baseline was $38\text{ mm}$, or inappropriately pass a patient whose baseline was $55\text{ mm}$ but who suffered severe post-traumatic limitation down to $41\text{ mm}$.
- **Rubric Correction & Proper Scope:**
  - **Status:** `COMPARATIVE CONTINUOUS OUTCOME — NO EXPLICIT NORMATIVE THRESHOLD REPORTED`.
  - **Rule Definition:** MIO must be evaluated as a continuous metric alongside lateral excursion and protrusion. In the absence of patient baseline records, clinical consensus commonly defines trismus as $\text{MIO} < 35\text{ mm}$, while values between $35\text{--}45\text{ mm}$ require dynamic assessment of deviation and joint pain rather than an arbitrary $40\text{ mm}$ cutoff.

#### Implication 4: FACE-Q Scores as Validation of a Composite Treatment-Outcome Score
- **Source Studies:**
  - Elegbede A, et al. (2018), *Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open*, 6(12):e2004 (`PAP-012` / `RES-EVD-012`).
  - Pusic AL, Klassen AF, et al. (2013), *Clin Plast Surg*, 40(2):249–260 (`PAP-013` / `RES-EVD-013`).
- **What the Primary Literature Actually Reports:**
  - Elegbede et al. (2018) prospectively administered six specific FACE-Q scales (Satisfaction with Facial Appearance, Recovery-Early Life Impact, Psychological Function, etc.) to 185 facial trauma patients, demonstrating high internal consistency (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.824\text{--}0.969$).
  - Pusic et al. (2013) developed the Satisfaction with Facial Appearance scale using Rasch Measurement Theory to construct a unidimensional, interval-level scale scored from $0\text{ to }100$.
- **Nature of the Overinterpretation:**
  - Attempting to claim that because FACE-Q scales are psychometrically validated, any algorithm or rubric that combines imaging metrics, bone gaps, occlusion grades, and FACE-Q into a single "composite treatment-outcome score" is also "validated" is a major methodological error.
  - FACE-Q scales measure distinct, patient-reported psychological and aesthetic constructs. They do **not** validate an external composite formula or weighting system that sums millimeters of displacement and survey points.
  - Conflating PROM scale validation with rubric validation creates an unearned veneer of clinical authority.
- **Rubric Correction & Proper Scope:**
  - **Status:** `CONSTRUCT-SPECIFIC PATIENT-REPORTED INSTRUMENT — CANNOT VALIDATE COMPOSITE RUBRICS`.
  - **Rule Definition:** The FACE-Q Craniofacial/Aesthetic modules provide a validated mechanism to measure the **patient-reported experience** as an independent outcome domain. In an evaluation architecture, PROMs must be reported as a separate dimension alongside anatomical and functional scores, rather than collapsed into an unvalidated arithmetic composite score.

### 4. Summary Matrix of Corrected Evidence Boundaries

| Clinical Domain | Candidate Parameter | Reported Source Grounding | What the Evidence SUPPORTS | What the Evidence CANNOT Support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ZMC Alignment** | 3D surface distance (MSD / RMSD) | Ho et al. 2016, 2017 ($n=15$) | Measuring 3D skeletal discrepancy against uninjured contralateral side; normal baseline asymmetry up to $\text{RMSD } 1.98\text{ mm}$. | Cannot support a claim that residual asymmetry of $\le 1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ mm}$ is a validated surgical success threshold. |
| **Orbital Reconstruction** | CT Volume Delta ($\Delta\text{mL}$) | Whitehouse 1994; Zhang 2012; Bly 2013 | $1.0\text{ mL} \approx 0.8\text{ mm}$ enophthalmos; computer guidance achieves $<1.0\text{ mL}$ delta; posterior floor herniation drives globe recession. | Cannot support a claim that $\Delta\text{mL} \le 1.0\text{ mL}$ is an independent, universal guarantee of surgical success. |
| **Mandibular Function** | Maximal Interincisal Opening (MIO) | Eckelt 2006 ($n=88$); Adeyemi 2012 ($n=60$) | ORIF achieves statistically greater MIO ($46.5\text{ mm}$ vs $40.9\text{ mm}$); 2 weeks IMF allows faster recovery of opening. | Cannot support a claim that $\text{MIO} > 40\text{ mm}$ is a universal binary threshold separating success from failure. |
| **Patient Satisfaction** | FACE-Q ($0\text{--}100$ Rasch score) | Elegbede 2018 ($n=185$); Pusic 2013 ($n>200$) | Reliable measurement of patient aesthetic satisfaction and early functional impact; smoking/MMF predict lower scores. | Cannot validate an arithmetic composite rubric combining imaging and clinical points. |
