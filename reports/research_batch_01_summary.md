# Research Evidence Batch 01 Summary Report

## Maxillofacial Fracture Treatment-Outcome Evaluator Project — Phase 3

### 1. Executive Summary
Phase 3 expands the evidence base from qualitative textbook descriptions to primary clinical research. A discovery batch of **14 high-impact studies** (10 clinical trials and observational cohorts, 2 systematic/developmental studies, 1 multicenter validation trial, and 1 landmark biomechanical model) was gathered, cataloged, and audited.

All 14 studies specifically target the evidence gaps identified in Phase 2:
- **100% Bibliographic Verification:** Complete citations, DOIs, PMIDs, and PMCID access links were retrieved and cross-checked against publisher and indexing metadata.
- **Strict Clinical Scope Adherence:** Every study directly informs postoperative evaluation (3D CT symmetry, orbital volumetry, mouth opening, dental occlusion, plate removal morbidity, validated PROMs, and inter-examiner reliability).
- **Zero Hallucination Standard:** No missing thresholds, weights, or scores were invented.

### 2. Summary of Research Evidence by Outcome Domain

#### Domain 1: Quantitative ZMC Skeletal Alignment & Facial Symmetry
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-001` / `RES-EVD-001`: Ho, Schreurs et al. (2016), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, PMID: 27591093.
  - `PAP-002` / `RES-EVD-002`: Ho, Schreurs et al. (2017), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, PMID: 29046241.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - Establishes 3D CT mirroring and surface-based iterative closest point (ICP) matching as the standard quantitative method for evaluating post-reduction ZMC symmetry.
  - **Normative Reference Baseline:** Ho et al. (2017) demonstrated that the uninjured adult zygoma has a natural baseline asymmetry of **$\text{Mean Surface Distance (MSD)} = 0.14 \pm 0.12\text{ mm}$** and **$\text{Root Mean Square Distance (RMSD)} = 0.87 \pm 0.21\text{ mm}$** (range up to $1.98\text{ mm}$).
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Provides the first objective anatomical benchmark: residual asymmetry below $\sim 1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ mm}$ falls within physiological variation and cannot be classified as surgical reduction failure.

#### Domain 2: Orbital Reconstruction, Volumetry, Enophthalmos & Diplopia
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-003` / `RES-EVD-003`: Whitehouse et al. (1994), *Br J Ophthalmol*, PMID: 7918289, PMC504888.
  - `PAP-004` / `RES-EVD-004`: Zhang et al. (2012), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, PMID: 21664740.
  - `PAP-005` / `RES-EVD-005`: Bly et al. (2013), *JAMA Facial Plast Surg*, PMID: 23328551.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - **Volumetric Conversion Ratio:** Whitehouse et al. established that **each $1.0\text{ cm}^3$ ($1.0\text{ mL}$) increase in orbital volume results in $0.8\text{ mm}$ of enophthalmos**.
  - **Anatomical Specificity:** Zhang et al. proved that total herniation alone is not the sole driver; orbital floor herniation **posterior to the vertical eyeball equator** is the primary anatomical determinant of clinically significant enophthalmos ($P < .05$).
  - **Surgical Accuracy:** Bly et al. demonstrated that computer-guided reconstruction achieves mean orbital volume discrepancies $<1.0\text{ mL}$ ($0.74\text{ mL}$ vs $2.12\text{ mL}$ freehand), significantly reducing late enophthalmos ($9\%$ vs $38\%$, $P = .04$).
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Directly links postoperative 3D CT volume restoration ($\Delta\text{mL} \le 1.0\text{ mL}$) to globe position preservation ($< 1.0\text{ mm}$ projection difference).

#### Domain 3: Mandibular Healing, Occlusal Alignment & Functional Recovery
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-006` / `RES-EVD-006`: Eckelt et al. (2006), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, PMID: 16781159 (Multicenter RCT).
  - `PAP-007` / `RES-EVD-007`: Ellis et al. (2000), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, PMID: 10716106.
  - `PAP-008` / `RES-EVD-008`: Adeyemi, Adeyemo et al. (2012), *J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, PMID: 22793957.
  - `PAP-009` / `RES-EVD-009`: Kroon et al. (1991), *J Craniomaxillofac Surg*, PMID: 1943807.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - **Maximal Interincisal Opening (MIO):** Eckelt et al. demonstrated that operative treatment (ORIF) produces significantly greater mean MIO ($46.5\text{ mm}$ vs. $40.9\text{ mm}$, $P = 0.001$), greater protrusion ($7.3\text{ mm}$ vs. $4.7\text{ mm}$), and lower terminal deviation ($19\%$ vs. $66\%$).
  - **Occlusal Predictability:** Ellis et al. established that closed reduction leads to significantly higher rates of malocclusion compared to open reduction across all initial displacement levels.
  - **IMF Duration Boundary:** Adeyemi et al. proved that **2 weeks of IMF** achieves identical bony union and occlusal stability to 4–6 weeks for minimally displaced tooth-bearing fractures.
  - **Biomechanical Grounding:** Kroon et al. demonstrated that single upper border miniplates fail to neutralize inferior border distraction, proving that dual-plane fixation is biomechanically mandatory.
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Defines objective functional recovery bounds: MIO $>40\text{ mm}$ as normal functional recovery, and dynamic occlusal concordance as an independent outcome criterion.

#### Domain 4: Postoperative Complications, Hardware Morbidity & Safety
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-010` / `RES-EVD-010`: Nagase et al. (2005), *Ann Plast Surg*, PMID: 16327461 (13-year cohort).
  - `PAP-011` / `RES-EVD-011`: Mosbah et al. (2003), *Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg*, PMID: 12615467.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - **Plate Removal Incidence:** Nagase et al. found a **$33.3\%$ overall removal rate**; $64.4\%$ of removals were driven by complications, with hardware palpability, cold intolerance, and pain accounting for **$72.4\%$ of complication-related removals**. Palpability concentrated over thin soft tissue envelopes (supraorbital, infraorbital, and mental foramina).
  - **Site Pain:** Mosbah et al. recorded a **$14\%$ incidence of localized post-surgical hardware pain**, with infection and wound dehiscence driving early hardware removal.
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Establishes that asymptomatic plates require no intervention, whereas hardware palpability over aesthetic facial units represents a distinct category of late surgical morbidity.

#### Domain 5: Validated Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs)
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-012` / `RES-EVD-012`: Elegbede et al. (2018), *Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open*, PMID: 30656106, PMC6326617.
  - `PAP-013` / `RES-EVD-013`: Pusic, Klassen et al. (2013), *Clin Plast Surg*, PMID: 23506765.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - Elegbede et al. prospectively validated six FACE-Q scales in **185 facial trauma fracture patients**, establishing **good-to-excellent psychometric reliability (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.824\text{--}0.969$)**.
  - Current tobacco smoking, mandibulomaxillary fixation, and Le Fort fractures were independent predictors of significantly lower postoperative satisfaction and quality of life.
  - Pusic & Klassen established the Rasch-transformed interval scoring system ($0\text{ to }100$, where higher equals superior outcome) for the core Satisfaction with Facial Appearance scale.
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Incorporates a validated, psychometrically defensible PROM into the evaluation framework, preventing reliance solely on surgeon radiographic grading.

#### Domain 6: Observer Agreement & Measurement Reliability
- **Selected Studies:**
  - `PAP-014` / `RES-EVD-014`: Mittermiller, Cornelius, Audigé et al. (2019), *Craniomaxillofac Trauma Reconstr*, PMID: 31719949, PMC6834479.
- **Key Evidentiary Findings:**
  - Multicenter evaluation of 200 CT scans by 15 surgeons demonstrated that **Level 2 fracture location variables have high interobserver reliability ($\text{median }\kappa = 0.69$, $94\%$ agreement)**, but **Level 3 morphology/displacement variables exhibit lower reliability ($\text{median }\kappa = 0.59$, $91\%$ agreement)**.
  - Surgical experience significantly increased reliability ($\kappa = 0.66$ high experience vs. $0.48$ low experience).
- **Outcome Rubric Role:** Cautionary evidence for rubric design: scoring rules relying on subtle fracture displacement or comminution morphology have lower inter-rater reproducibility and require standardized, explicit measurement instructions.
